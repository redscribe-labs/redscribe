# Commercial License

RedScribe is **source-available**, not [OSI-approved open source](https://opensource.org/osd)
— the source is public and modifiable, but the license restricts *who* can
use it for free based on the nature of the use. That puts RedScribe in the
same category as projects like Sentry (Functional Source License), n8n
(Sustainable Use License), Chatwoot, and MariaDB (Business Source License).
We say this plainly because we don't want anyone assuming OSI open source
terms apply when they don't.

## The two tracks

**Personal / community / noncommercial use — free.**
Covered by the [PolyForm Noncommercial License 1.0.0](LICENSE): individuals,
students, hobbyists, charitable organizations, educational and public
research institutions, and anyone using RedScribe for a noncommercial
purpose. Full functionality, no feature gap, no time limit.

**Commercial use — requires this commercial license.**
If you or your organization uses RedScribe in the course of commercial
activity — including a for-profit pentest/security consultancy using it to
manage paid client engagements — you need a commercial license, regardless
of how many people use it or how many engagements you run.

There is no feature difference between the two tracks. Nothing in RedScribe
is disabled, watermarked, or gated behind payment. The commercial license
exists to cover the *right to use it commercially*, not to unlock anything.

## Pricing

**Free for the first year.** As a kickstart for the community, a commercial
license key costs nothing right now, valid for up to twelve months from
issue. Request one the same way described below.

Priced tiers begin once RedScribe has been proven at the scale real
enterprise engagements demand — flat rate, no per-seat, per-user, or
per-engagement charges, one organization, one price, regardless of team
size. Nothing about a key already issued during the free period changes
retroactively once pricing starts.

## Support

Commercial licensees may request support on a best-effort basis. This is
**not** a guaranteed-response-time (SLA) support contract — it's priority
attention when we're available, not a service-desk commitment.

## Why not just use AGPL, or make it fully permissive?

A permissive license (MIT/Apache-2.0) would let anyone — including
well-funded competitors — repackage and resell RedScribe without
contributing anything back. AGPL's network-use copyleft mostly doesn't
bite for a tool run internally by a firm (it triggers on offering modified
source to the software's own users over a network, which rarely applies to
internal engagement-management tooling), so it wouldn't actually produce
the "commercial users pay" outcome we want. PolyForm Noncommercial draws
the line directly on the axis we care about: personal/community use stays
free forever, commercial use funds ongoing development.
